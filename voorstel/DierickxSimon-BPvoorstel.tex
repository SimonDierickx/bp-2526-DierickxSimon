\begin{abstract}
  Modern society has developed an immense dependence on existing
  telecommunications infrastructure, which renders emergency communication
  inherently vulnerable. The same events that trigger crisis
  situations---war, natural disasters, cyberattacks, power outages, and
  censorship---disable these networks at the exact moment they are most
  needed. Off-grid alternatives exist, but each presents significant
  barriers in terms of cost, deployment, or usability in an emergency
  context. APRS and DMR require operator licences and specialized hardware
  configuration, while commercial satellite messengers impose per-device
  costs and subscription fees, making large-scale deployment for volunteer
  and municipal organizations nearly impossible. Meshtastic, an open-source
  LoRa-based mesh networking platform, operates in a licence-free frequency
  band, is low-cost compared to alternatives, and requires no SIM card,
  internet connection, or operator licence. While some evidence of its use
  exists from small-scale community deployments, no systematic evaluation of
  Meshtastic's reliability or deployability in an emergency context has been
  published. This thesis therefore investigates the following research
  question: \textit{To what extent does Meshtastic, as a decentralized
  LoRa-based mesh network, offer a reliable and readily deployable emergency
  communication solution when primary network infrastructure fails?} The
  research proceeds through five phases: a structured literature study; a
  Proof of Concept (PoC) design phase; execution and measurement of the PoC,
  collecting RSSI, SNR, packet delivery rate, and end-to-end latency data
  across both direct-link and relay-chain topologies; a security analysis;
  and a structured comparative analysis. The expected outcome is a set of
  empirically grounded performance benchmarks under real-world Belgian
  deployment conditions. The thesis is expected to conclude with a qualified
  answer to the research question, identifying the conditions under which
  Meshtastic could serve as a viable emergency communication solution.
\end{abstract}
